Cameron Farvin
About

Now
Cameron is a doctoral student in computer science at The University of Southern Mississippi. His work sits at the intersection of machine learning, political theory, and philosophy: he studies how AI alignment architectures such as reinforcement learning from human feedback, Constitutional AI, and rule-based guardrails encode ethical and political commitments, and what those engineering decisions mean for national security, corporate political speech, and democratic governance.

He came to the doctorate by an unusual route. After eight years as a professional software engineer, Cameron returned to school and completed a Bachelor of Arts in philosophy and a Bachelor of Science in political science at Southern Miss in December 2025, earning a 4.0 GPA in both majors. Along the way he was named Outstanding Student in Political Science and received the Forrest Wood Philosophy Endowment Scholarship, the William Colmer Political Science Scholarship, and departmental recognition for best papers in his coursework.

Then
Cameron served as an infantryman in the Indiana Army National Guard while completing his first bachelor's degree, in physics, at Indiana University Bloomington. After graduating he taught himself computer programming with the help of Harvard University's free online courses. As a software engineer he contributed to space and defense programs at NASA's Stennis Space Center, the National Air and Space Intelligence Center, and DARPA through Radiance Technologies, and to engineering efforts at Northrop Grumman's Xetron.

He then moved into computer graphics and performance, teaching himself the fundamentals of the field and working as a senior software engineer on GPU drivers, tooling, and optimization at Advanced Micro Devices and Intel Corporation. While working full time at Intel he completed three semesters of master's coursework in economics with a 4.0 GPA.

In early 2021 Cameron left engineering and his economics studies to study philosophy and political science. He wanted to understand how democratic institutions hold together, how they fail, and what obligations fall to the people who build the technologies those institutions increasingly depend on.

Next
Cameron is looking for work that puts technical depth in the service of policy. He is most useful where engineering detail actually determines the political stakes, in AI governance, national security technology, and the institutional questions raised by systems whose values are set at design time.

Honors & Appointments

Cameron received the EAGLE SPUR Research Grant, a competitive university research award, for his work on the conditions under which democratic institutions become most fragile precisely when they are most necessary. He presented that research at the Mississippi Philosophical Association's 2024 annual conference, and related research at the Concerned Philosophers for Peace 2024 annual conference, where it received the Outstanding Paper award.

Cameron was selected as a fellow for the Hertog Foundation's Fall 2024 humanities seminar on the life and work of Benjamin Franklin. He served as co-editor for the Concerned Philosophers for Peace, completed an internship at Northwestern University investigating ethics dumping in international research, and contributed his software skills to the USM Center for Digital Humanities' Riot Acts Project.
